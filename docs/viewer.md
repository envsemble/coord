# Viewer and layout

## Viewer behavior

Scroll or pinch to zoom slowly and smoothly. Drag the background to pan. Hover or selection dims unrelated elements; a relation keeps only itself and its two endpoints highlighted. The active entity, relation or inheritance branch takes envUI's selection blue; connected elements take a lighter tint of it. Arrowheads, aggregation diamonds, relation labels and counts follow the active relation's color. In a proposal, a changed element keeps its change colour and shows focus with a ring or halo in the same blue. A selected element has a slightly thicker outline. Controls provide immediate hover, pressed, focus and open/selected feedback. Selection survives navigation, theme changes and switching layers. Clicking the background or pressing Escape clears it.

Scrolling, dragging, pinch gestures, zoom buttons and Fit share one frame-synchronized camera animation. Input accumulates without dropping movement, zoom stays anchored under the cursor, and drag follows more tightly than zoom. The grid shares the diagram's world origin and zoom; neighboring dot densities blend smoothly without gaps after large or negative pans. Reduced-motion preferences apply movements immediately.

Each association shows the counts written at its ends. A relation shows its label when it has one: a captured type use is a dashed arrow without a label, and a foreign key shows its columns. Inheritance uses a hollow triangle and implementation a dashed one, with no counts. Routes have only horizontal and vertical segments, checked during generation. The viewer follows the system's light or dark theme; the theme button switches it, remembered when browser storage permits. Embedded in Atlas, it follows Atlas's theme. The offline viewer's SVG, PlantUML and ZIP downloads (the ZIP with every export, its evidence and validation) work directly from disk.

The grid canvas fills the whole viewer, in the page's own background, its elements surfaces on it as envUI's cards are on a page; every control floats over it in islands built from envUI components (pill surfaces, icon buttons with tooltips and Material Symbols), in envUI's light and dark themes. The top-left island holds the host's content, the view switcher (only when there is more than one view) and, for proposals, the **Changes** button with its count and the toggle that fades unchanged elements. The top-right island holds zoom, Fit, **Reset layout** (once the reader has arranged the diagram) and **Understand the model** (the scenarios that exemplify a class diagram, each assessed), plus theme, exports and help in offline exports. A host with navigation of its own (`sections`, see [embedding](embedding.md#the-react-panel)) puts it first, on the top row with the tools; the navigation island then takes the row below, and in narrow containers the tools take a third. Where the model came from (generated from code or a database, proposed by an agent or authored by an agent) is the host's to show; the offline viewer shows it beside the model's title, with the source, capture date and coverage as its tooltip and in **About**. Elements always show their attributes and methods; no stereotype is drawn. Controls use icons with tooltips. Press `+`/`−` to zoom and `0` to fit. Fit fills the area left between the floating controls, enlarging small diagrams up to 150%. When the islands no longer fit on their rows, the view switcher first becomes a dropdown, then text labels hide, and finally the zoom step buttons hide; the toolbar returns to its full form when space allows. A proposal's **Changes** and the scenarios open in a side panel of their own, from their buttons: choosing a change, or stepping to the previous or next one, selects it on the canvas and frames it. In narrow containers the panel spans the width. Opening it pans the camera only when it would cover the selected element.

In the Atlas app, a capture, the later captures compared with it and the proposals over it are one diagram with layers: the first capture, the later captures by date, then the proposals by title. They are laid out together, so switching layers moves nothing. The Files tree is how a reader moves between them: the first capture's row opens like a folder onto its later captures and proposals (by their file names, which the agent chooses, each with a small tag, Captured, its date or Proposal, where there is room), the one shown marked; `[` and `]` step to the previous and next layer too, wrapping round. Above the diagram, the plan's breadcrumb leads back to the plan. Each switch is a navigation, so Back returns to the layer before.

An element can be dragged to where the reader wants it; its relations follow, routed around the other elements. When it is let go, the whole arrangement as shown (every element's place, by its id) is saved beside the family's first file, `orders.puml` → `orders.layout.json`, for all its layers. An element without a saved place, such as one added since, goes beside the elements it relates to, at the nearest free place. **Reset layout** removes the arrangement and lays the diagram out again. An element's or member's description is its tooltip on the canvas.

Pointer highlights commit immediately, with no hover debounce. A new highlight is visible immediately at 70% opacity and settles over 80 ms; the cached background dims or restores over 100 ms. These brief fades are disabled for reduced motion. Diagram geometry is memoized, adjacency is indexed once per immutable scene, and highlights draw a small overlay above a cached transparent SVG image. The SVG retains accessible interaction targets and vector fallback rendering. Rounded zoom updates reach only their readout; panning reuses the grid's raster settings. While an element is dragged, only the relations it disturbs are routed again, quickly (keeping off other routes by cost alone); when it is let go they are routed in full, so a route may settle into a better place. Pass `backgroundURL` to `DiagramCanvas` or `UMLDiagram` when embedding a matching transparent SVG image in a custom UI; the complete viewer creates it automatically. Replace scene/model objects when changing their contents.

During development, open `/performance.html` for an independent fixture with 1,000 classes, 1,996 relations, and eight attributes per class. Its 24-sample hover benchmark reports input-to-highlight and React render timings. Use `?nodes=2000` to vary the size. It is excluded from the offline application. On the development machine, the 1,000-class highlight median is about 3.4 ms (95th percentile 4.5 ms), and the React render median 1.8 ms. Timings vary with browser, hardware and workload.

A diagram is laid out as people draw one: in groups of related elements, with room between them. A diagram's `package` blocks are its groups, each drawn as a quiet region with its name; without packages, the groups are found from the relations by modularity (a deterministic Louvain clustering, in which a generalization ties its ends most, then a composition or an association, and a dependency least), a hierarchy stays in one group, and a lone element or a pair joins the group it relates to most. Groups found so are used only when they hold the relations: when more than a fifth of them would run between groups (as with a type every class uses), the diagram is laid out in one piece. A diagram of six elements or fewer is one group. Each group is laid out on its own as below, the groups are packed beside each other (the more related nearer), and the relations between groups are routed around every element, as the longer lines. Routes keep off each other: a route avoids running along another (the ends on a side stay a port apart, and a full side gives way to the next), crossing it, and passing through its labels, and a label goes where it covers the fewest other labels and lines.

Within a group, the generic layout engine compares three deterministic candidates: the existing layered layout and two alternatives using ELK's [edge-length compaction](https://eclipse.dev/elk/reference/options/org-eclipse-elk-layered-compaction-postCompaction-strategy.html). It favors shorter total relation length, with smaller penalties for bends, crossings and occupied area. A compact candidate cannot add crossings, shared association paths or obscured labels relative to the reference. All candidates must pass node, label and orthogonality checks. Connected entities can move closer while their identities, association ends, multiplicities and generalization sets remain unchanged. No domain-specific rules enter this algorithm. Candidate metrics and rejection reasons are included in the validation export. This is a bounded layout heuristic, not a guarantee of the global minimum for every graph.

Exports have one compressed copy in the HTML; SVG downloads use the chosen theme. The included page is about 45% smaller than the initial uncompressed library build. Source repositories and the layout engine are build-time inputs, never runtime network dependencies.
