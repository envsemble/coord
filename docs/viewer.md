# Viewer and layout

## Viewer behavior

Scroll or pinch to zoom slowly and smoothly. Drag to pan. Hover takes priority over selection in the details panel. Hover or selection dims unrelated elements; a relation keeps only itself and its two endpoints highlighted. The active entity, relation or inheritance branch takes envUI's selection blue; connected elements take a lighter tint of it. Arrowheads, aggregation diamonds, relation labels and counts follow the active relation's color. In a proposal, a changed element keeps its change colour and shows focus with a ring or halo in the same blue. A selected element has a slightly thicker outline. Controls provide immediate hover, pressed, focus and open/selected feedback. Selection survives navigation, theme changes and field toggling. Clicking the background or pressing Escape clears it.

Scrolling, dragging, pinch gestures, zoom buttons and Fit share one frame-synchronized camera animation. Input accumulates without dropping movement, zoom stays anchored under the cursor, and drag follows more tightly than zoom. The grid shares the diagram's world origin and zoom; neighboring dot densities blend smoothly without gaps after large or negative pans. Reduced-motion preferences apply movements immediately.

Each association shows its known counts at both ends; captures record unknown counts, which are left off. A relation's label shows its name and stereotype only when they say something the line does not: a captured type use is a dashed arrow without a label, an OntoUML relation without a name of its own shows only its stereotype, and a foreign key shows its constraint name (from SQLite, which names none, its columns). Inheritance uses a hollow triangle and no counts. Routes have only horizontal and vertical segments, checked during generation. The viewer follows the system's light or dark theme; the theme button switches it, remembered when browser storage permits. Embedded in Atlas, it follows Atlas's theme. Entity backgrounds distinguish kinds (blue), roles (orange), relators (purple), phases (green), events (red) and modes (cyan) in both themes and SVG exports. Source snippets include line numbers and pinned GitHub links. SVG, standard JSON, evidence, views, notes, validation and ZIP downloads work directly from disk.

The grid canvas fills the whole viewer; every control floats over it in islands built from envUI components (pill surfaces, icon buttons with tooltips and Material Symbols), in envUI's light and dark themes. The top-left island holds the host's model picker, view tabs (only when there is more than one view) and, for proposals, the **Changes** button with its count and the toggle that fades unchanged elements. The top-right island holds concept search, the attribute toggle, zoom, Fit, **Understand the model** (OntoUML lessons, checks and open questions, and any walkthroughs and examples the document has) and the details toggle (plus theme, exports and help in offline exports). A provenance label at the bottom left states whether the model was generated from code or a database, proposed by an agent or authored by an agent, with the source, capture date and coverage. Controls use icons with tooltips; descriptions and learning explanations expand on demand. Press `/` to focus concept search, `+`/`−` to zoom and `0` to fit. Fit fills the area left between the floating controls, enlarging small diagrams up to 150%. When the islands no longer fit on one row, view tabs first become a dropdown, then search collapses to a button with an anchored field and text labels hide, and finally the zoom step buttons hide; the toolbar returns to its full form when space allows. In narrow containers the details panel spans the width. Selecting an element, or reopening details, pans the camera only when the details panel would cover the element. Details float on the right below the toolbar; use the details toggle or its close button to hide them without clearing selection, and they stay hidden while hovering, selecting, panning, zooming or switching views. Reopening shows the hovered element, otherwise the selection; with neither, no details are shown, or a proposal's list of changes. Understand the model explicitly opens the guide. Clicking the background or pressing Escape still clears selection.

An element's details put first what helps judge it: in a proposal, what changes (each added, changed or removed member, with before → after values, or where a new declaration is proposed to live, decoded from its identity); where it is in code (file, lines and the source excerpt; each member's line); why it exists (its rationale and open questions, then the planning passages linked to it, each a two-line excerpt that opens the plan at its section); and what it holds (fields and methods, or a table's columns, keys and definition). OntoUML elements keep their checks and stereotype lessons. Evidence status shows only when it qualifies an element (inferred or unresolved). Capture diagnostics are not repeated on elements: the provenance label states the capture's coverage, and the diagnostics stay in the document's capture record.

Pointer highlights commit immediately, with no hover debounce. A new highlight is visible immediately at 70% opacity and settles over 80 ms; the cached background dims or restores over 100 ms. These brief fades are disabled for reduced motion. An open inspector follows the same input commit; only opening a hover-only sidebar waits 100 ms to avoid covering a click target. Diagram geometry is memoized, adjacency is indexed once per immutable scene, and highlights draw a small overlay above a cached transparent SVG image. The SVG retains accessible interaction targets and vector fallback rendering. Rounded zoom updates reach only their readout; panning reuses the grid's raster settings. Pass `backgroundURL` to `DiagramCanvas` or `UMLDiagram` when embedding a matching transparent SVG image in a custom UI; the complete viewer creates it automatically. Replace scene/model objects when changing their contents.

During development, open `/performance.html` for an independent fixture with 1,000 classes, 1,996 relations, and eight attributes per class. Its 24-sample hover benchmark reports input-to-highlight and React render timings. Use `?nodes=2000` to vary the size. It is excluded from the offline application. On the development machine, the 1,000-class highlight median fell from about 401 ms to 3.4 ms (95th percentile: 467 ms to 4.5 ms); React render median fell from 120 ms to 1.8 ms. Timings vary with browser, hardware and workload.

The generic layout engine compares three deterministic candidates: the existing layered layout and two alternatives using ELK's [edge-length compaction](https://eclipse.dev/elk/reference/options/org-eclipse-elk-layered-compaction-postCompaction-strategy.html). It favors shorter total relation length, with smaller penalties for bends, crossings and occupied area. A compact candidate cannot add crossings, shared association paths or obscured labels relative to the reference. All candidates must pass node, label and orthogonality checks. Connected entities can move closer while their identities, association ends, multiplicities and generalization sets remain unchanged. No R5 names or domain-specific rules enter this algorithm. Candidate metrics and rejection reasons are included in the validation export. This is a bounded layout heuristic, not a guarantee of the global minimum for every graph.

Exports have one compressed copy in the HTML; SVG downloads use the chosen theme. The included page is about 45% smaller than the initial uncompressed library build. Source repositories, the layout engine and Shiki are build-time inputs, never runtime network dependencies.
